Supplementary material
Subject-independent forearm sEMG electrode reduction with cross-fitted attribution
and non-inferiority testing
This supplement provides detailed methods, exploratory analyses and participant-level results for
the electrode-reduction study. Full-precision CSV files accompany the result tables and figures. Raw
recordings are available from the public dataset repositories identified in the main article.
S1 Analysis design and scope
The primary endpoint is participant Macro-F1, evaluated against a 0.02 engineering tolerance using
a participant-bootstrap percentile interval. The analysis design followed pilot work and was not
independently preregistered. Model-budget calibration used outer-fold outcomes for participants 1 and
7. Count-feature thresholds were calibrated from pooled recordings before participant partitioning;
exclusion of individual participants did not change the rounded thresholds. These development
exposures limit prospective interpretation despite participant-disjoint model fitting, scaling and
ranking.
Secondary test families and the participant-level aggregation, full geometric count curve, shared-
resource comparison, gesture analysis, fixed-layout analyses and Monte Carlo sensitivity are exploratory
or post-hoc. The full geometric curve was motivated by the twelve-electrode comparison. Selector
activity is a post-hoc descriptive analysis of the penalty results. These analyses are not independent
prospective validation. No independently collected cohort with the same GRABMyo montage was
available.
S2 Signal processing and feature definitions
The primary active cache contains 563,472 windows. Participant exclusion reproduced the rounded
monopolar threshold in 43 of 43 folds and the DB5 threshold in 10 of 10 folds. This numerical
invariance does not imply fold-specific threshold calibration. Amplitude units and quantized DB5
values are distinct. Rest-inclusive and alternate-window features are extracted from raw recordings
without crossing trial boundaries.
For DB5, the filter function limits the upper cutoff to min(frequested, 0.45fs − 1), yielding an
effective passband of 10–89 Hz. The band-pass design argument is four, giving eight poles before
forward–backward application. GRABMyo’s 450 Hz upper limit is below the clipping cap.

S3 Inference and test families
All intervals resample paired participant scores, not individual windows. Their estimand is the
conditional mean out-of-fold performance change. Inner and outer models share training participants,
so neither 42 attribution vectors nor 43 outer fits are independent fitted-model replicates. The
bootstrap does not refit the entire nested procedure.
The primary count decision uses the percentile lower bound. The percentile-tail Holm calculation
uses unshifted bootstrap differences and is not a null-generated test. The null-centred studentized
sensitivity constructs null residuals dp − ¯d, resamples them, studentizes the resampled mean and
compares it with ( ¯d + δ)/(sd/√n); the Monte Carlo probability is (1 + # {tb ≥t obs})/(B + 1). A
family contains all fifteen reduced counts for one condition, margin and penalty. At a tested fixed
count, the unadjusted interval remains a separate descriptive criterion. RF tests only twelve against
sixteen and estimates nok ∗.
Post-hoc secondary families contain eight attribution-versus-random tests, twelve fold-aggregation
penalty tests and fifteen deletion tests. The participant-extension comparison family contains sixty
tests (four penalties times fifteen counts). Its penalty diagnostic has twelve tests; the shared-resource
comparison has two; the per-gesture analysis has sixteen. Geometric12 is exploratory, with a direct
paired method contrast as well as each method’s change from full16. Wilcoxon tests concern signed
ranks; percentile intervals concern the mean, so their decisions need not coincide. In DB5, count
decisions also depend on the bootstrap construction: at the 0.03 margin, the percentile-tail Holm
calculation supports twelve, while the null-centred studentized Holm sensitivity supports no reduced
count. This disagreement limits the small-cohort inference.
S4 Participant and gesture predictions
Full16, SHAP12 and geometry12 predictions use matched outer-test observations and true labels. The
entire gesture analysis uses matched models under the primary 300-round, 200,000-window protocol.
Per-gesture F1 is computed for every participant before inference; confusion matrices are normalized
by true gesture within participant before averaging. Thus large window counts do not inflate the
inferential sample size.
Gesture numbers follow the explicit numeric order in MotionSequence.txt; gesture 14 is forearm
pronation. Participant-loss summaries are descriptive rather than individual non-inferiority tests.
Correlation of a full-array score with its own difference is mathematically coupled and is not interpreted
causally.
S5 Participant-level attribution and selector activity
For each outer participant, the post-hoc extension takes the median and unscaled MAD across the 42
normalized individual attribution vectors. It preserves all fixed penalties and counts. Person vectors
share five attributing models and reflect both person heterogeneity and model differences. Their
dispersion does not provide independent fitted-model replicates.
A post-hoc descriptive analysis quantifies whether each fixed penalty changes the unordered
physical site set relative to its unpenalized counterpart. It covers every count and participant for
both aggregation rules and reports changed-set counts and mean Jaccard similarity. Identical site
sets produce identical classifier inputs under the fixed protocol; identical scores in this case are not
evidence of equivalence between different layouts.
S6 Resources, sensitivity and fixed-layout validation
The reduced-budget selection methods used a common final lighter model but unequal ranking
resources. RBF-SVM uses C = 1 and the scikit-learn scale rule for γ; RF uses 200 trees, minimum
leaf size two and no depth limit. The RF sensitivity retains those parameters. Stored feature values
are float32; feature standardization uses the appropriate training-pool mean and population SD plus
10−8, preserving the distinction between full inner-training scaler pools and capped final-model fit
samples. The resource-matched comparison shares each inner model and its validation observations.
Electrode-group permutation preserves within-electrode feature blocks and permutes only within

the median across inner folds after normalization; SHAP and permutation remain different scoring

Rest-inclusive and 150/500 ms analyses cover every count and participant and retain the primary

The RF experiment holds the XGBoost-selected layout fixed and compares twelve with sixteen using
these RF parameters; it is not a RF ranking or count-optimization study.
Fixed-layout rankings use the five-fold median/MAD rule within development data. Pooled
feature thresholds still apply. Development and test participants are disjoint; their inner and final
models use all development windows, not the primary curve’s fixed training cap. Count selection
already used the full cohort. Odd-development and even-development derive different layouts. Their
reverse-direction result cannot be portrayed as validation of the first layout. A post-hoc Monte
Carlo analysis repeated the percentile bootstrap under 200 fixed randomizations, each using 10,000
resamples; the reverse layout qualified in 90 repetitions. This diagnoses numerical decision sensitivity
rather than the probability that the hypothesis is true. DB5 and derived bipolar analyses use their
own references; no channels or participants are pooled across datasets or representations.
